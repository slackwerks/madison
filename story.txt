# The Sparrow's Glass Prison

Marcus the sparrow had always been too curious for his own good. That Tuesday morning, while hopping along the park fence, he spotted a discarded soda bottle glinting in the grass, its amber glass still glistening with sugary residue. The sweet scent was irresistible. Without a second thought, he thrust his head deep into the bottle's neck, his tiny tongue lapping eagerly at the sticky coating inside. But when he tried to pull back, his head wouldn't budge—# Marcus the Sparrow

Marcus was not the brightest sparrow in the flock. While his brothers and sisters learned to find worms in the garden and seeds in the bird feeder, Marcus was usually found investigating shiny objects or trying to befriend the neighborhood cat. His mother often sighed and said, "That boy has more curiosity than sense."

One Tuesday afternoon, Marcus spotted something wonderful near the recycling bin—a glass bottle that smelled faintly of sweet apple juice. Without thinking (as usual), he stuck his entire head inside to lick the last drops. That's when he realized his head fit in perfectly, but getting it back out was another matter entirely. He began to panic, flapping and stumbling around the yard, bumping into the fence, the garden gnome, and finally the bottl-

e bird bath with a tremendous splash. The cold water shocked him so badly that he gave one mighty, desperate pull—and pop! His head came free, sending the bottle rolling across the lawn. Marcus sat there soaking wet and gasping, his feathers sticking up in all directions, looking absolutely ridiculous.

From that day forward, Marcus's mother noticed a change in her son. He still investigated interesting things, but now he paused first, tilted his head, and seemed to actually *think* before acting. Well, most of the time. Just last week she caught him trying to squeeze through the gaps in the garden trellis, but at least he'd learned to back out when things got tight. "There's hope for that boy yet," she chirped to herself, watching Marcus successfully extract himself and fly away with his dignity barely intact.
